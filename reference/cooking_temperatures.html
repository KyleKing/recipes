Cooking Temperatures

Safe minimums from the USDA safe temperature chart

Personal rating: Not yet rated

No image yet

Every figure is the temperature at the thickest part, read with an instant-read thermometer and away from bone. The recommended line is what we actually cook to, which for whole-muscle beef, pork, and salmon sits below the USDA minimum by choice. Carryover keeps climbing 3-5 °F while the meat rests, so pull it early.

Fish and Shellfish

Salmon

  • 120 °F rare, translucent through the center
  • 130 °F medium-rare (recommended)
  • 140 °F medium, firm and flaking
  • 145 °F USDA safe minimum, fully opaque

Farmed salmon carries enough fat to forgive a few degrees past medium-rare. Wild sockeye is lean and dries out fast, so pull it nearer 120 °F.

White Fish

  • 130 °F barely set, still glossy
  • 137 °F just flaking (recommended)
  • 145 °F USDA safe minimum

Cod, tilapia, and halibut have no fat to hide overcooking, so the window between flaking and chalky is only a few degrees.

Tuna

  • 115 °F rare, cool red center (recommended)
  • 125 °F medium-rare
  • 145 °F USDA safe minimum, gray throughout

Searing a tuna steak past medium-rare wastes it. Canned tuna is already cooked and needs no temperature.

Shrimp

  • 120 °F just opaque, still tender (recommended)
  • 145 °F USDA safe minimum

Shrimp go from tender to rubbery in under a minute. The shape is a better cue than the thermometer: a C is done, an O is overcooked.

Poultry

Chicken Breast

  • 150 °F pasteurized after roughly 3 minutes held, still juicy (recommended)
  • 157 °F pasteurized on contact
  • 165 °F USDA safe minimum, instant

Pasteurization is time at temperature, not a single number. Breast held at 150 °F for 3 minutes is as safe as breast that touched 165 °F, and it is noticeably less dry.

Chicken Thigh

  • 165 °F USDA safe minimum
  • 180 °F connective tissue rendered (recommended)

Dark meat is full of collagen that only breaks down well past done, so a thigh pulled at 165 °F is safe but chewy.

Ground Turkey

  • 165 °F USDA safe minimum (recommended)

Grinding spreads surface bacteria through the meat, so ground poultry has no range below the minimum.

Beef and Pork

Steak

  • 120 °F rare
  • 130 °F medium-rare (recommended)
  • 140 °F medium
  • 150 °F medium-well
  • 160 °F well done

Skirt and flank are thin enough that carryover overshoots easily, so pull them 5 °F under the target.

Beef for Braising

  • 145 °F USDA safe minimum
  • 203 °F collagen fully rendered, shreds with a fork (recommended)

A braise is not cooked to safe, it is cooked to tender. Chuck and stew meat need hours above 190 °F before they stop being tough.

Pork Shoulder

  • 145 °F USDA safe minimum
  • 203 °F pulls apart (recommended)

Like beef chuck, shoulder is a collagen cut and the safe minimum is nowhere near done.

Ground Beef

  • 160 °F USDA safe minimum (recommended)

Ground Pork

  • 160 °F USDA safe minimum (recommended)

Notes

  • An instant-read thermometer disagreeing with the oven dial is right and the dial is wrong
  • Carryover cooking runs 3-5 °F in a fillet or chop and 10 °F or more in a roast, so the number to aim for is always below the number you want
  • Reheated leftovers and anything with a sauce go to 165 °F regardless of what the raw ingredient wanted
