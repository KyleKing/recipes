Baking Powder and Baking Soda Substitutions

How They Differ

Baking soda is a base and needs an acid in the batter (buttermilk, yogurt, brown sugar, molasses, cocoa, citrus, vinegar) to release gas. Baking powder already carries its own acid, so it lifts on its own. Double-acting baking powder releases once when it gets wet and again in the oven.

Substitutions

Baking Powder for 1 tsp Baking Soda

  • 3 tsp baking powder

Baking soda is about three times stronger, and that much powder can leave a bitter, salty edge. Working the other way around is the safer swap.

Baking Soda for 1 tsp Baking Powder

  • 1/4 tsp baking soda
  • 1/2 tsp cream of tartar
  • 1/4 tsp cornstarch or tapioca starch

This mixture is single-acting, so it lifts on contact with liquid and nothing else. Bake the batter right away.

Notes

  • Buttermilk swapped in for regular milk brings acid with it, so add 1/4 tsp baking soda to use it
  • Both lose strength on the shelf: drop baking soda in vinegar or baking powder in hot water and look for immediate fizzing
